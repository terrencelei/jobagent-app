# Cover letter template

The structure and rules every cover letter draft follows before it is personalized to a posting.
His general letter, with the experience modules filled in from his resume and profile, is
`private/cover-letter-base.md`. Start every draft from it, then personalize.

Researched 2026-10-22 from engineering career centers and hiring managers (sources at the end). No
published mechanical or hardware letter with a verified interview outcome was found. These rules
come from career-center samples and hiring managers' stated preferences, not from proven letters.

## Shape: one page, about 280 to 350 words, four paragraphs

1. **Hook (50 to 70 words).** Name the role. In the first sentence or two, give one specific,
   factual reason this company: its hardware, vehicle, product or problem, taken from the posting
   or the company's own description, and tied to something he has actually done. Follow with a
   one-line identity from the profile: year, major(s), school and graduation date (e.g. "an economics
   senior at Example State University (May 2027)"). Preview the two qualifications the next paragraphs prove.
2. **Proof 1 (90 to 110 words).** The experience that best matches the posting's top requirement.
   Give the problem, what he built or analyzed, a number where his sources have one, and the
   outcome. Use the posting's own words for the work (an ATS and the reader both match them).
3. **Proof 2 (80 to 100 words).** A second experience that shows hands-on building or system
   integration, mapped to a second requirement. Add the statistics major only when it does work
   (test data, failure analysis), never as a credential.
4. **Close (35 to 50 words).** The tools and shop skills the posting names that he has. One
   sentence on what he would own or contribute there. A request to talk, and thanks.

Greeting: "Dear <Company> Hiring Team," (never "To whom it may concern" or "Dear Sir/Madam").
Sign-off: "Sincerely," then the student's full name on its own line. The approved text is the whole letter,
from greeting to sign-off. jobagent prints it as a one-page PDF under his resume letterhead (name and
contact line, Times) with the date, and uploads it to the cover-letter field.

## Rules

- **His learned style first.** `private/writing-style.md` (learned from his edits) overrides anything
  here it contradicts.

- **Explain, don't list.** Say what each experience taught him or proves, and point it at their
  work. Cut any sentence any engineering student could have written.
- **No passion or flattery boilerplate.** Banned: "I have always been fascinated by",
  "passionate about", "excited by your mission", "I am confident I would be a great asset",
  "driving impactful results", "innovative". Specific facts carry the enthusiasm.
- **Two or three experiences, not five.** Choose by the posting (modules below). Leave the rest to
  the resume.
- **Numbers only from his sources.** For example 0.1 to 100 g at 0.01 g resolution, 1000+ tools,
  patent pending, 50k tests a day. Never estimate or round up.
- **Facts only from his resume, tailored copies and `private/profile.json`.** Capstone work is
  the team's; his own contribution is the ROS 2 and xArm setup guides. Do not claim the employer's
  product domain (e.g. orbital vehicles, vehicle closures) unless he has worked on it.
- **Hands-on and first principles** are what hardware startups (SpaceX, Anduril, Varda) say they
  look for. Prefer a thing he built and tested over a responsibility he held.
- **Check before handing it over:** company and role names correct, nothing left from another
  employer, 280 to 350 words, fits one page.

## Experience modules (pick by the posting)

Match the posting's emphasis to the profile: lead with the role or project whose skills and summary
best cover the posting's top requirement, and use a second one for the next requirement. Respect each
role's and project's caution (what the student did not do). Never borrow an experience the profile
does not list.
